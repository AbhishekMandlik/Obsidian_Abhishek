### Why Reliability Matters
- Users expect systems to be always available consistent and trustworthy.
- Downtime costs money, reputation and user trust.
- High Reliability = low failure + fast recovery.
System will fail, Reliability is how gracefully they handle it.

### What is System Reliability?
Reliability is the ability of a system to operate continuously without failure. 
It includes:
1. Correctness.
2. Consistency.
3. Fault Tolerance.
4. Uptime.

### Key Metrics - MTBF and MTTR
1. Mean-time Between Failures: Average time a system works before failing.
2. Mean Time to Recovery: Average time to recover from a failure.
High MTBF + Low MTTR = High Reliability.

### What are Service Level agreements?
1. SLAs are contractual guarantees about system performance.
2.  Common SLA metrics:
	1. Availability.
	2. Response time
	3. Error Rate
3. 99.9% uptime= 8.76 hours of downtime a year.

### Availability vs Durability
A: System is accessible and responsive
D: Data is safe and not lost.
A protects user experience while D protects user Data.

### Impact of Reliability on System Design
Design Decision that affect reliability:
1. Redundancy(multiple instance, failover)
2. Health checks and monitoring.
3. Retry mechanism and circuit breakers. 
4. Distributed design patterns(replication and quorum)

### Reliability in Distributed Systems
1. Challenges:
	1. Network partitions.
	2. Node failures.
	3. Eventual Consistency.
2. Solutions:
	1. Use CAP-aware design.
	2. Ensure fault isolation.
	3. Implement replication and consensus algorithms(paxos and raft).
### Reliability in Cloud-Native Systems
- Cloud infra is inherently unreliable.
- Design for:
	- Transient failures.
	- Auto-scaling and self healing.
	- Chaos Engineering to test resilience.
Design for failure is the cloud-native mindset.
