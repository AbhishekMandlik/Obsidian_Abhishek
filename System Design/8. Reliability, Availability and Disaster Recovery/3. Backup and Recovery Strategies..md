- Backup: Creating copies of data to protect against loss.
  Why is it important:
	- Hardware or software failure.
	- Human error.
	- Cyber attacks(ransomware).
	- Natural disaster.
	- Compliance and data retention.
- Recovery: Restoring data from backups after failure or corruption.
Critical for disaster recovery, ransomware protection, hardware failures. It compliments replication and redundancy.

### Types of Backup
1. Full Backup
	1. Copies all data.
	2. Simple recovery
	3. High storage cost and time.
2. Incremental Backup
	1. Backs up only changes since last backup.
	2. Small, fast but needs all increments to restore.
3. Differential Backup
	1. Backs up changes since last full backup.
	2. Bigger than incremental, faster restore.\
	3. Here you need only last full backup + incremental back ups after that to restore the system, In between both of the above.
### Recovery Types (Cold, Warm, Hot)
1. Cold Recovery:
	1. Backups stored offline.
	2. High Downtime, low cost.
2. Warm Recovery:
	1. Some resource pre-provisioned.
	2. Moderate downtime and cost.
3. Hot Recovery:
	1. Fully redundant, always ready.
	2. Minimal downtime, highest cost.

### RTO and RPO
1. Recovery Time Objective:
	1. How quickly can a system recover?
2. Recovery Point Objective:
	1. How much data loss is acceptable.

Shorter RTO/RPO = Higher Cost and more complexity

### Trade-offs in Backup Strategy
- Cost vs Recovery Speed.
- Complexity of managing backup frequency and retention.
- Balance based on:
	- Business criticality.
	- Compliance Needs.
	- Infrastructure maturity.
### Best Practices
1. Automate backups and testing of restores.
2. Encrypt backups at rest and in transit.
3. Monitor backup success and failures.
4. Apply 3-2-1 Rule:
	1. 3 Copies.
	2. 2 Different mediums.
	3. 1 Offsite.
