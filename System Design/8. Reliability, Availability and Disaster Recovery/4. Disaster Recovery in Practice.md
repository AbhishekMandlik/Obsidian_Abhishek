### Why Disaster Recovery Matters?
- Downtime Costs -- especially for mission-critical systems.
- Protects against regional outages, data loss and cyber attacks.
- Complements backup strategies with full system-level resilience.
- Required for compliance in regulated industries.

### DR for mission-critical Applications
1. Systems are designed around strict RTO and RPO target. Resilience across every layer of the stack and not only replicated DBs but redundant compute, storage, networking and often multiple availabilities of region.
2. Automation: Automated failover + tested recovery plans.
3. Examples: banking, healthcare and e-commerce platforms.

### Failover + Backup = True Resilience
1. Backups alone = data recovery.
2. Failover = service continuity.
3. Combine both:
	1. Backups for corruption or deletion.
	2. Failover for infra or region outage.
4. Include both in DR plan for full coverage.

### Testing and Automation
- DR plans must be tested regularly.
- Automate:
	- Failover switching.
	- Data Validation after restore.
	- Notifications and logging of every service.
- Run DR drills: simulate failures.
- If you have not tested it, you don't have it.

### Geo - Redundancy and Quorum-Based Design
1. Geo-Redundancy: Deploy across multiple physical locations
2. Quorum-Based design: Quorum is the minimum number of servers that must be acknowledged to be successful before marked as success.
3. This Ensures
	1. Data Consistency.
	2. Safe Failover.

### Challenges in Geo-Distributed Systems:
1. Data Consistency across regions.
2. Latency during sync and failover. It adds communication overhead.
3. Regulatory constraints(data locality). Countries data in the same country.
4. Co-ordinating multi-region failovers.
